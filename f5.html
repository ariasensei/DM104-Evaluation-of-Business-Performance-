Week 1: Value Creation, ROI, and Performance Analytics

ILO: Assess value creation using performance and financial metrics.

Core Concept: What is Value Creation?

Value creation is when a business generates more worth than the resources it consumes. Think of it simply: if you spend ₱100 and generate ₱150 in benefits, you created ₱50 in value.

Key Metrics You Must Know

ROI (Return on Investment)

ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) × 100

Real Example: Jollibee invests ₱5M in a new drive-thru system. After 1 year, it generates ₱7M in additional revenue with ₱1M operating costs.

ROI = (7M - 5M - 1M) / 5M × 100 = 20%

EVA (Economic Value Added)

EVA = NOPAT - (Capital × Cost of Capital)

Real Example: SM Retail earns ₱10B after tax, uses ₱80B capital at 10% cost.

EVA = 10B - (80B × 0.10) = ₱2B positive value created

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)

Real Example: Grab Philippines tracks: average delivery time (target: <30 min), driver rating (target: 4.5+), order completion rate (target: 95%)

Balanced Scorecard (BSC)

Four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth

Example: Globe Telecom measures subscriber growth (financial), NPS score (customer), network uptime (process), employee training hours (learning)

Performance Analytics Framework

Performance analytics converts raw data into actionable insights. The cycle:

Collect Data → Measure KPIs → Analyze Trends → Generate Insights → Take Action

Real World: Netflix

Netflix tracks "viewing completion rate" as a KPI. If 80% of viewers finish a show's first episode but only 40% finish episode 3, analytics tells them they have a mid-season engagement problem. They used this to decide to invest in stronger episode 2-3 cliffhangers, resulting in 15% higher series completion rates and reduced churn (subscribers canceling).

Financial vs Non-Financial Metrics

Financial Non-Financial
Revenue Growth Rate Customer Satisfaction (CSAT)
Profit Margin Employee Engagement Score
Return on Assets (ROA) Net Promoter Score (NPS)
Cost per Acquisition Brand Awareness %

📝 Week 1 Takeaways for the Exam

  • Value creation = benefits generated exceed resources consumed
  • ROI tells you efficiency; EVA tells you if you're beating your cost of capital
  • KPIs must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
  • Balanced Scorecard prevents tunnel vision on just financial metrics
  • Analytics without action is just expensive data collection

Week 2: Risks, Bias, and Data Quality Issues

ILO: Evaluate risks and limitations in performance measurement systems.

Big Idea

"Not everything that counts can be counted, and not everything that can be counted counts." — William Bruce Cameron. Performance metrics can mislead if we don't understand their limitations.

Types of Measurement Risks

1. Gaming the Metrics (Goodhart's Law)

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

Real Example — Wells Fargo Scandal (2016): Employees were given KPIs of "8 accounts per customer." To hit targets, staff opened 3.5 million fake accounts without customer consent. The metric worked perfectly — the behavior it incentivized was catastrophic. Fine: $3 billion.

2. Survivorship Bias

Real Example: A business school studies "successful startups" and finds they all used aggressive marketing. Conclusion: aggressive marketing = success. Problem: They didn't study the 90% of startups that ALSO used aggressive marketing and still failed. You're only looking at survivors.

3. Confirmation Bias

Real Example: A manager believes their new training program works. They cherry-pick data from the top 20% of trainees who improved, ignore the 40% who showed no change, and present only favorable metrics to leadership. The dashboard "confirms" success because they selected only supporting evidence.

4. Data Quality Issues

The 5 Data Quality Dimensions:

  • Accuracy — Is the data correct? (e.g., typo: revenue of ₱10M entered as ₱100M)
  • Completeness — Are there missing values? (e.g., 30% of survey responses blank)
  • Timeliness — Is data current? (e.g., using 2019 data for 2024 decisions)
  • Consistency — Same data across systems? (e.g., CRM says 500 customers, finance says 480)
  • Relevance — Does it measure what matters? (e.g., tracking page views when goal is sales)

Correlation ≠ Causation

Classic Trap: A company notices that stores with more employees have higher revenue. Conclusion: "Hire more people = more revenue!" Reality: Stores in high-traffic malls naturally need more staff AND naturally have more revenue. The location drives both — not the headcount.

⚠️ Always ask: "Is there a lurking variable explaining both?"

Mitigation Strategies

For Gaming

  • Use multiple balanced metrics
  • Include qualitative assessments
  • Rotate KPIs periodically
  • Reward behaviors, not just outcomes

For Data Quality

  • Automate data validation
  • Regular data audits
  • Clear data entry standards
  • Cross-reference multiple sources

📝 Week 2 Takeaways for the Exam

  • Goodhart's Law: targets corrupt the metrics they're based on
  • Always ask "what am I NOT seeing?" (survivorship bias)
  • Data quality has 5 dimensions — memorize them
  • Correlation ≠ causation — look for lurking variables
  • Best defense: triangulate with multiple metrics and qualitative data

Week 3: Ethics, Data Governance, and Responsible Analytics

ILO: Demonstrate ethical and responsible use of performance data.

Why Ethics Matter in Analytics

Data is power. How organizations collect, analyze, and act on performance data can help OR harm employees, customers, and society. Ethical analytics isn't optional — it's a competitive advantage and legal requirement.

Core Ethical Principles in Performance Analytics

1. Transparency

People should know what's being measured and why.

Real Example — Amazon Warehouse Workers: Amazon uses AI to track worker productivity per second. Workers reported not knowing exactly how algorithms scored them, leading to firings they couldn't understand or appeal. In 2022, California passed AB 701 requiring companies to disclose productivity quotas to warehouse workers.

2. Fairness & Non-Discrimination

Real Example — Amazon's AI Hiring Tool (2018): Amazon built an AI to screen resumes. It was trained on 10 years of hiring data — which was mostly male hires. The AI learned to penalize resumes containing "women's" (e.g., "women's chess club captain"). Amazon scrapped the tool, but the lesson remains: biased historical data creates biased performance systems.

3. Privacy & Consent

Real Example — Philippines Data Privacy Act (RA 10173): A BPO company installs keystroke logging and webcam monitoring on employee laptops without informing them. This violates RA 10173 because employees weren't given proper notice or consent. Penalty: up to ₱5M fine and imprisonment.

4. Accountability

Real Example — Facebook/Meta (2021): Internal documents showed Facebook knew Instagram harmed teenage mental health (their own performance data showed it), but they suppressed findings because engagement KPIs were positive. Whistleblower Frances Haugen exposed this — demonstrating that organizations must be accountable for what their data reveals.

Data Governance Framework

Data governance = the rules, roles, and processes for managing data responsibly.

Component Purpose Example
Data Ownership Who is responsible? HR owns employee data; Marketing owns customer data
Access Controls Who can see what? Only managers see individual performance; execs see aggregates
Retention Policies How long do we keep it? Employee reviews kept 5 years post-employment
Quality Standards How accurate must it be? Financial data: 99.9% accuracy; survey data: 95%
Audit Trails Who changed what, when? All KPI target changes logged with justification

Responsible Analytics Checklist

Before deploying any performance measurement system, ask:

  • ✅ Necessity: Do we actually need this data to make the decision?
  • ✅ Consent: Do people know they're being measured?
  • ✅ Proportionality: Is the intrusiveness justified by the benefit?
  • ✅ Bias Check: Could this disadvantage any protected group?
  • ✅ Appeal Process: Can someone challenge a data-driven decision about them?
  • ✅ Expiration: When will this data be deleted?

📝 Week 3 Takeaways for the Exam

  • Ethical analytics = transparent, fair, private, and accountable
  • Philippines Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) governs data collection
  • Algorithmic bias comes from biased training data
  • Data governance has 5 components: ownership, access, retention, quality, audits
  • Always apply the "newspaper test" — would you be comfortable if this practice was on the front page?

Week 4: Business Performance Case Study

ILO: Integrate analytics and KPIs in a real-world business case.

Case Study: Shopee Philippines — From Growth to Profitability

We'll analyze how Shopee shifted from a "growth at all costs" strategy to profitability, examining which KPIs they changed and why.

Background

Shopee (owned by Sea Limited) dominated Southeast Asian e-commerce through massive subsidies: free shipping, seller vouchers, and aggressive marketing (celebrity endorsements, flash sales). By 2022, investors demanded profitability. Sea Limited's stock dropped 80% from its peak.

The Dilemma: How do you shift from measuring "Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)" to measuring "profitability" without killing the business?

Phase 1: Growth KPIs (2019–2021)

KPI Target Result
GMV (Gross Merchandise Value) ↑ 50% YoY Achieved (but at massive losses)
Monthly Active Users ↑ 30% YoY 343M users by Q3 2021
Orders per User ↑ quarterly Grew but driven by subsidies
Market Share #1 in SEA Achieved in 6 countries

⚠️ Problem: GMV was vanity metric — it included subsidized orders that were unprofitable.

Phase 2: Profitability Pivot (2022–2024)

New KPI Why It Matters Action Taken
Adjusted EBITDA Measures actual operating profit Cut marketing spend 50%
Take Rate Revenue per transaction Increased seller commissions 2% → 4%
Cost per Order Operational efficiency Reduced free shipping, consolidated logistics
Contribution Margin per Order Profit per unit economics Removed unprofitable product categories

Result:

Sea Limited reported its first-ever profitable quarter in Q4 2022. By shifting KPIs from growth to profitability, they went from -$931M loss (Q3 2021) to +$422M profit (Q4 2022) in just 5 quarters.

Analysis Framework: How to Approach Any Case Study

Step 1: Context

What's the business? What stage? What external pressures exist?

Step 2: Identify KPIs

What are they measuring? Are metrics aligned with strategy?

Step 3: Evaluate Quality

Are there biases? Gaming? Data quality issues?

Step 4: Recommend

What should change? Why? What are the risks of your recommendation?

📝 Week 4 Takeaways for the Exam

  • KPIs must evolve with business strategy — what got you here won't get you there
  • Vanity metrics (GMV, page views, downloads) can mask real problems
  • Unit economics (profit per order/customer) reveal true health
  • Shifting KPIs has consequences — Shopee lost users but gained profitability
  • Use the 4-step framework: Context → KPIs → Quality → Recommendations

Week 5: Final Project Presentation & Evaluation

ILO: Present and justify performance evaluation findings and recommendations.

🎯 FINAL PROJECT: Performance Analytics Audit

Individual or Duo | Presentation: 12–15 minutes + 5 min Q&A

Choose ONE real company (local or international) and conduct a full performance analytics audit.

Project Deliverables

Part 1: Company Profile & Strategy (15%)
  • Company overview (industry, size, market position)
  • Current business strategy (growth? profitability? transformation?)
  • Identify at least 3 stakeholder groups affected by their performance metrics
Part 2: KPI Identification & Analysis (25%)
  • Identify 5–8 KPIs the company currently uses (from annual reports, news, investor presentations)
  • Classify each: financial vs non-financial, leading vs lagging
  • Map KPIs to a Balanced Scorecard framework
  • Calculate ROI or EVA for at least one major initiative (use real or estimated numbers)
Part 3: Risk & Bias Assessment (25%)
  • Identify at least 2 potential biases or risks in their measurement system
  • Analyze data quality concerns (use the 5 dimensions)
  • Find at least 1 example of potential metric gaming or misalignment
  • Provide evidence (news articles, reports, logical analysis)
Part 4: Ethics & Governance Review (20%)
  • Evaluate: Is their data collection transparent? Fair? Privacy-respecting?
  • Identify any ethical concerns in how they use performance data
  • Assess their data governance maturity (basic/developing/advanced)
  • Reference relevant laws (RA 10173, GDPR, industry-specific regulations)
Part 5: Recommendations & Justification (15%)
  • Propose 3–5 specific, actionable recommendations
  • For each: explain the expected impact, implementation difficulty, and timeline
  • Justify using concepts from Weeks 1–4
  • Address potential risks of your own recommendations
